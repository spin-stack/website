An installation has two kinds of cost: a small one that runs all the time, and runners, which
run while workspaces do and for a while after (`runner_idle_minutes`). Once its runner is given back,
a stopped workspace costs what it keeps in the bucket.
Prices depend on the region and change; every price here says where and when it was read, and
the [AWS Pricing Calculator](https://calculator.aws/) has yours.

## Three months, priced

The module's default machines, on demand, in the regions Spin OS has images in. The machines are
at the prices the AWS pricing page listed on {published} (the record was written on {generated},
and the daily sync rewrites it); disks, addresses, keys and zones at AWS's price lists of
2026-11-19:

These are list-price estimates. Runners are spot by default, so a runner usually costs less than

logs, and what leaves the region, at EC2's price per GB; they depend on what your workspaces do.

## With nobody working

| What | Default | List price, us-east-1 and us-west-2 (2026-11-19) |
|---|---|---|
| Control plane | One machine, [`instance_type`](/reference/terraform/controlplane/#instance_type), which also runs the database | In the table above |
| Proxy | One machine, [`proxy_instance_type`](/reference/terraform/controlplane/#proxy_instance_type) | In the table above |
| Their disks | gp3: the control plane's [`root_volume_gb`](/reference/terraform/controlplane/#root_volume_gb) and the proxy's 20 GB | $0.08 per GB a month |
| Public IPv4 addresses | Two: the control plane's, and the proxy's elastic IP | $0.005 an hour each |
| KMS keys | Three: the installation's encryption, identity and volume keys | $1 a month per key version |
| Route 53 | Two hosted zones | $0.50 a month each |
| S3 | What the volumes, the database's archive, logs, documents and certificates hold | S3 Standard, $0.023 per GB a month |
| CloudWatch | The flow log and the machines' boot logs, <Value name="logRetentionDays" /> | Cents a month at a few hosts |

There is no NAT gateway, no load balancer and no interface endpoint: every machine has its own
public address, and the bucket is reached through a gateway endpoint, which is free.

At list price that is the table's first row, before the buckets and the logs. The one
measurement so far is higher: on a day the module's own install test left an installation
standing in us-east-1, the region's bill was $2.34 (2026-11-01), about $70 over a month. That day's
bill may include the test itself, and it was not broken down: one data point, not a figure for
your installation. Your own is on the [Costs page](#reading-the-bill),
as the standby.

## While workspaces run

Runners are the largest cost, and they cost only while they are up. By default a runner is a
spot machine with local NVMe, of 32 to 48 vCPUs: a c8id, m8id or r8id 8xlarge or 12xlarge
([`runner_instance_types`](/reference/terraform/root/#runner_instance_types),
[`runner_vcpus`](/reference/terraform/root/#runner_vcpus)). Each also has a public IPv4 address
and a gp3 root disk.

[The control plane sizes the runners' group](<feature:Runners start when a workspace waits for one and stop when nothing runs>):

- It starts the first runner when a workspace waits for one, and adds another only when a
  workspace has waited ten minutes, up to `max_runners`.
- When no workspace has run, started or waited for
  [`runner_idle_minutes`](/reference/terraform/root/#runner_idle_minutes), it empties the group,
  and at once during `quiet_hours`.
- While workspaces run on other runners, it gives back a runner that has held nothing only it
  has for as long, one at a time.

[Runners and hosts](/concepts/runners/#scaling-from-zero) has the details. A workspace nobody uses
is suspended, and so is one that runs past the installation's limit, so a forgotten workspace
does not keep a runner up all night ([Workspaces](/concepts/workspaces/)).

How many workspaces fit on one runner is not measured yet. A workspace's share of a runner is
what it held, not what it used: the control plane divides the runners' cost by the cores and
memory each workspace held while it ran ([The bill](/admin/operations/#the-bill)). Runner
capacity nobody held is shown apart, as idle.

### Spot

AWS prices spot by zone and hour. Over the 30 days to 2026-11-19, the default runner types were
at these prices an hour, against the on-demand list:

| Type | On demand | Spot, us-east-1: median (low to high) | Spot, us-west-2: median (low to high) |
|---|---|---|---|
| c8id.8xlarge | $1.77 | $0.53 ($0.34 to $1.06) | $0.61 ($0.47 to $0.89) |
| c8id.12xlarge | $2.66 | $0.78 ($0.52 to $1.11) | $0.79 ($0.67 to $1.20) |
| m8id.8xlarge | $2.09 | $0.72 ($0.47 to $1.32) | $0.75 ($0.62 to $1.05) |
| r8id.8xlarge | $2.66 | $1.00 ($0.67 to $2.13) | $1.02 ($0.77 to $1.43) |

The control plane makes a group for each host class, and the default class's asks for all six types the defaults match (the four above, and m8id and r8id 12xlarge), with the price-capacity-optimized strategy, in every
zone the installation has a subnet in (up to
[`availability_zones`](/reference/terraform/controlplane/#availability_zones)), and with Capacity
Rebalancing on; a runner it takes away is held for
[`runner_drain_seconds`](/reference/terraform/controlplane/#runner_drain_seconds) while it empties. On 2026-11-19,
AWS's spot placement score for those six types together was 9 of 10 for one runner in both
regions, and in 5 of the 9 zones asked one zone at a time. For more runners it held in us-west-2
(9 at 10 and at 20 runners) and fell in us-east-1 (5 at 10, 2 at 20). AWS gives a low score to
any request that names fewer than three types, so ask for the set. Scores change from day to day,
and they say how likely AWS is to start spot capacity, not how long it keeps it.

```bash
aws ec2 describe-spot-price-history --region us-east-1 --product-descriptions Linux/UNIX \
  --instance-types c8id.8xlarge c8id.12xlarge --start-time 2026-10-20T00:00:00Z
aws ec2 get-spot-placement-scores --region us-east-1 --region-names us-east-1 --target-capacity 1 \
  --instance-types c8id.8xlarge c8id.12xlarge m8id.8xlarge m8id.12xlarge r8id.8xlarge r8id.12xlarge
```

When AWS has no spot capacity, the group keeps asking for it. A host class can be set to fall
back instead: the control plane starts the group's new runners on demand for 30 minutes, then asks for spot again;
runners already started on demand stay until they are given back. It is off for the default
class and on only where a class turns it on (Admin → Runners), and each switch is in the audit
log.

[A runner AWS reclaims suspends its workspaces to the bucket](<feature:A cloud's reclaim warning empties the host before the machine goes>)
within the notice AWS gives, and they resume on another runner
([When a runner goes away](/concepts/runners/#when-a-runner-goes-away)). Whether a full runner's
memory fits in that notice is not measured yet.

## A stopped workspace

Once its runner is gone, a stopped or suspended workspace costs only what it keeps in the
volumes bucket, at S3 Standard's price: $0.023 per GB a month for the first 50 TB, in us-east-1
and us-west-2 (2026-11-18). A suspended one also keeps its memory checkpoint there, at most its
whole RAM. Clones share their image's layers, so a hundred workspaces from one image store the
image once. Its owner sees what keeping it stopped costs a month, on its page.

The bucket keeps every object under Object Lock for
[`object_lock_days`](/reference/terraform/controlplane/#object_lock_days) and removes it the day
after. So a deleted workspace is paid for twice over: in the trash, a week by default, with its
disk; then, once it is erased, its bytes, checkpoints included, for about 31 days more by
default.

## Reading the bill

The control plane reads the installation's own bill: [the month's costs from Cost Explorer, its
forecast, and its budget if it has one, every six hours](<feature:The month's bill, its forecast, and what each workspace cost>).
A day's cost settles a day or two after it ends. `spin admin costs` and the dashboard's Costs page
(Admin) show it, and [The bill](/admin/operations/#the-bill) is how to read it and how the month is
shared out. On AWS, two things have to be in place first.

The bill is read by the installation's tags, and AWS shows a tag in the bill only once it is a
cost allocation tag. That is the account's to switch on, once, and AWS refuses to before it has
seen the tag on a resource: after the installation has run for a day, apply bootstrap with
`cost_allocation_tags = true`, in one bootstrap per account. Until then the Costs page is empty.

`monthly_budget_usd` makes an AWS Budget over everything the installation tags, and mails
`budget_emails` at 80% and 100% of what it cost and at 100% of the forecast. Nothing is stopped
when it is passed.

## Settings that change the cost

| Setting | Effect |
|---|---|
| `runner_idle_minutes` | How long runners stay up once nothing runs, and how long a spare one is kept |
| `quiet_hours`, `time_zone` | A window, such as nights, when runners go as soon as nothing runs |
| `max_runners` | The most runners at once |
| `runner_spot = false` | On-demand runners: more expensive, never reclaimed |
| `runner_instance_types`, `runner_vcpus` | Smaller or larger runners |
| `instance_type`, `proxy_instance_type` | The size of the control plane and the proxy |
| `flow_logs`, `dns_query_logs`, `log_retention_days` | What is kept in CloudWatch: the flow log is on by default, the DNS query log off |
| `monthly_budget_usd` | A budget and its alerts; it stops nothing |
